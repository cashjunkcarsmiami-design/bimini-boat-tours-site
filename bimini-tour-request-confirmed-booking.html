BIMINI TRIP PLANNING

From a Bimini Tour Inquiry to a Confirmed Booking

October 19, 2026

Sending a preferred date and group size starts a conversation. It does not by itself confirm a boat, a meeting point or an itinerary. For a private Bimini outing, keep a short written record of what the team has offered and which questions still need answers. This helps everyone in the group make plans around the same information.

Separate a request from an accepted itinerary

Ask whether the proposed date and vessel are available and how long an offer remains valid. Confirm the passenger count, planned duration and where the outing begins and ends. An activity shown on the website is an idea to discuss, not confirmation that it will be part of your particular booking.

Review the complete price and terms

Request the total and an explanation of inclusions, exclusions and any payment schedule. Ask how cancellations, changes and weather decisions are handled. Confirm who is providing each part of the trip and which arrangements are separate. Do not assume a private outing in Bimini includes transportation from Florida, a hotel or a transfer to the dock.

Keep one final version for the group

Once the team confirms the booking process is complete, share the meeting instructions and contact details with the people traveling. Keep superseded proposals separate so an old time or location is not mistaken for the final plan. If the guest count or travel arrangements change, notify the team and obtain updated confirmation before relying on the previous itinerary.
