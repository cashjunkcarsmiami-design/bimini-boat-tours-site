Questions to Ask Before a Bimini Snorkeling Tour

A snorkeling request should account for guest experience, equipment, water conditions and the type of stop being considered.

Describe every guest's swimming comfort

Tell the operator how many guests are confident swimmers, beginners or non-swimmers. Do not treat one experienced person as proof that the entire group is prepared. Ask whether the requested activity is appropriate for children, older travelers and anyone with mobility or health concerns.

Guests should follow crew instructions and use required safety equipment. A person who feels uncomfortable should not be pressured to enter the water.

Ask what equipment is included

Confirm whether masks, snorkels, fins and flotation devices are available, which sizes are offered and whether guests may bring personal equipment. A poorly fitting mask or fin can affect comfort. Ask how equipment is cleaned and when sizing is handled.

Understand that conditions can change

Wind, waves, current, visibility and marine activity can change the location or timing of a snorkeling stop. A photograph from another day does not guarantee identical water conditions. Ask how the operator evaluates conditions and what alternatives may be discussed.

Review the site's Bimini weather guide and prepare a backup priority such as sightseeing, beach time or a different protected-water stop.

Clarify the type of snorkeling experience

Ask whether the request involves shallow calm water, a reef area, a shipwreck view or another environment. Depth, entry method and water movement can differ. Guests should not assume that every stop is suitable for every ability level.

If the group is interested in the Sapona, compare the Sapona shipwreck tour information with a standard snorkeling or sandbar request before choosing priorities.

Protect the marine environment

Do not touch coral, chase wildlife, feed animals or remove shells and natural objects when prohibited. Follow local rules and crew guidance. Maintain distance from marine life and avoid standing on sensitive underwater areas.

Plan for sun, hydration and motion

Ask what shade and drinking water are available and bring suitable sun protection. Travelers with medical or motion concerns should obtain individualized advice from a qualified professional before the trip.

Share the group's departure details

Guests using Miami departure planning, Fort Lauderdale planning or West Palm Beach planning should send the preferred date, backup date, group size and snorkeling experience when requesting availability.

Snorkeling FAQ

Is clear visibility guaranteed?

No. Visibility and water conditions change and cannot be guaranteed for a future date.

Can non-swimmers participate?

Ask the operator about the requested trip and available alternatives. A non-swimmer should not enter the water without appropriate approval, supervision and equipment.

Will we see specific wildlife?

Wildlife sightings are unpredictable and should not be guaranteed.

Can the stop change?

Yes. Weather, safety, traffic and local conditions may affect the route or activity.

Additional planning questions · November 2, 2026

Discuss getting back aboard as well as entering the water

Ask about the entry and exit arrangements on the proposed vessel and how the crew organizes the requested activity. Explain relevant access needs privately before making a booking decision. A photo of a ladder or another group does not establish that every participant can use the arrangement. Ask about the options for guests who choose to stay aboard and confirm the actual vessel details when the plan is accepted.

Keep equipment requests specific

Provide the number of people who wish to participate and ask which equipment and sizes can be supplied. Confirm whether personal gear is permitted and when fitting questions are resolved. Do not infer that a pictured item is included or that extra equipment is always available. If a particular requirement is essential, get an answer before paying rather than leaving it as an assumption in the group chat.

Prepare the alternative activity question

Tell the operator what the group would prefer if the requested snorkeling stop is unsuitable and ask what could realistically be offered. Separate a changed activity from a cancelled outing when reviewing terms. Keep transportation to Bimini distinct unless it is expressly included in the accepted proposal. A submitted inquiry does not guarantee a boat, snorkeling conditions or a particular wildlife encounter.

Clarify what participation means for each guest

Before choosing a Bimini snorkeling outing, say who wants to enter the water, who prefers to watch and what experience guests want the operator to consider. Ask about the equipment supplied for the proposed activity, available sizes and the instructions given before entry. Do not assume that every guest must participate or that a boat photograph establishes a particular support arrangement. Let the operator explain the available options and any participation requirements for this booking.

Ask about comfort between water activities

For guests remaining aboard or taking a break, ask about seating, shade, changing arrangements and storage on the actual boat. Find out which personal items the operator recommends and which refreshments, if any, are included. A snorkeling stop does not establish that other activities or food are included. If a group member needs a specific arrangement, explain it before confirming and ask whether it can be accommodated; keep private personal details out of public booking comments.

Separate snorkeling participation from being a passenger

Tell the operator who intends to snorkel, who is uncertain and who prefers to remain aboard. Ask how the proposed outing accommodates that mix and what instructions each guest should receive. A place on the boat does not establish that an activity suits every participant. Discuss relevant access or support questions privately before accepting the plan.

Confirm the actual equipment arrangement

Ask which snorkeling items are supplied, whether sizes need to be reserved, what guests may bring and how equipment availability is confirmed. Do not infer included gear from promotional photographs. Share the operator’s current instructions with everyone and ask what changes if snorkeling cannot proceed as proposed. Follow crew instructions rather than attempting an activity based on a guide alone.
